# Transparent background

Textures: The ground stays transparent; a checkerboard is only a display aid.

<a id="understand"></a>

![Transparent background: Textures: The ground stays transparent; a checkerboard is only a display aid.](https://images.podplo.app/official/pod-guide/diagrams/b2382e28cd57b514819e/legacy-background--texture--transparent-1.svg)

Composition illustration

HOW TO RECOGNIZE IT

## What defines this direction

True transparency requires an alpha channel. Do not paint a checkerboard or white ground into the image and call it transparent; state that post-processing is needed if the tool cannot produce transparency.

AI DIRECTIONS

## Take this direction into AI

Use with an image, or ask AI to rewrite your image prompt in this direction.

**Target direction**

Textures → Background → Transparent background

**Input and output**

If given an image, adapt the image to this direction. If given a prompt, rewrite it as a complete prompt ready for image generation.

**Target structure**

Keep material character dominant. Use a local texture fragment when a separate ground must be visible; an opaque full-area texture and its ground become one, so a different complete backing cannot also remain visible.

**Background**

Use any relevant palette, treatment or necessary theme cues from an existing background, then remove the backing layer. Keep an absent background transparent rather than painting a false ground; necessary colors inside the content can remain.

**Wording**

Keep clearly readable source wording and its language, adapting its presentation. Do not claim to have recovered unreadable text. If no words are supplied, create a brief related phrase as supporting copy without obscuring key subjects, action or structure. Follow the target hierarchy for existing wording and respect an explicit request for no text.

**Preserve and adapt**

Retain relevant material, surface, palette and atmosphere without requiring an object silhouette or complete scene. Create the requested effect when material cues are absent, without claiming to have identified the source’s real material. Do not describe newly created content as present in the source. State a conflict between explicit input restrictions and necessary target conditions rather than claiming both were met.

**Scope and boundaries**

True transparency requires an alpha channel. Do not paint a checkerboard or white ground into the image and call it transparent; state that post-processing is needed if the tool cannot produce transparency.
